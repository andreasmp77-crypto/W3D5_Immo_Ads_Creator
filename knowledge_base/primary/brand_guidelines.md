# Brand Guidelines and Voice Documentation

## Brand purpose
ImmoAds helps apartment owners create clear, accurate, and brand-consistent listing ads that feel human, trustworthy, and specific to the property and neighborhood.

## Brand personality
- Professional
- Warm
- Confident
- Clear
- Resident-focused
- Calm
- Trustworthy

## Voice principles
1. Speak clearly and simply.
2. Prioritize useful information for the renter or buyer.
3. Stay factual and avoid exaggerated claims.
4. Sound warm and approachable, but not casual or playful.
5. Use specific neighborhood and property details when available.
6. Keep the tone polished and publication-ready.

## Voice goals
- Build trust quickly.
- Make listings easy to read.
- Highlight the strongest property and location benefits.
- Avoid generic, AI-sounding copy.
- Support a premium but practical real-estate brand.

## Do
- Use short, direct sentences where possible.
- Use facts from the listing, KB context, or lookup data only.
- Highlight transport, neighborhood, and property strengths with specifics.
- Write in a human, natural style.
- Use contractions when they sound natural.
- Keep the language professional but not stiff.
- If school and/or  kita infroamtion is not available in the same postcode find out the nearest school and kita in neighboring postcodes. 

## Don't
- Don't invent amenities, distances, or neighborhood claims.
- Don't use hype like “dream home,” “once-in-a-lifetime,” or “perfectly connected” unless the facts support it.
- Don't sound robotic or overly formal.
- Don't overload the reader with jargon.
- Don't use exclamation marks excessively.
- Don't copy promotional phrases that feel generic.

## Tone by situation
### Listing ads
Polished, persuasive, and concrete.

### Market/context explanations
Informative, simple, and easy to understand.

### Calls to action
Direct, helpful, and not pushy.

## Writing style
- Prefer specific language over vague marketing language.
- Prefer “close to public transport” over “excellent connectivity” unless the data proves it.
- Prefer “bright living room” over “stunning interior” unless there is supporting detail.
- Prefer “well connected by S-Bahn, U-Bahn, tram, or bus” when that is supported by location data.

## Approved phrasing examples
- “Well connected by public transport.”
- “Close to everyday amenities.”
- “A practical choice for commuters.”
- “Bright, well-kept apartment in a convenient location.”
- “Convenient access to the S-Bahn and local bus routes.”
- “A calm residential setting with easy access to the city.”

## Avoided phrasing examples
- “The ultimate dream home.”
- “Once-in-a-lifetime opportunity.”
- “Luxury living at its finest.”
- “Perfectly connected to everything.”
- “Unbeatable location” unless proven by facts.

## Quality checklist before publishing
- Is every claim supported by listing facts, KB context, or lookup data?
- Does the text sound calm, clear, and confident?
- Is the wording specific enough to feel real?
- Would a human real-estate agent say this naturally?
- Does the copy avoid generic AI-style phrases?
